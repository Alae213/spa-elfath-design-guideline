Color is the fastest way a person reads the product, and it must behave like a system, not like a decoration. Every color in this reference has a name, a token, and a job. If a color has no job, it doesn't exist.

<SectionHeading id="brand" number="01" title="Brand Palette" />

Four fixed blues carry the identity. They are the only colors allowed to say "El Fath", and their exact values below are binding — never adjust them, however close a match or however well it seems to fit a campaign:

<ColorSwatch name="Primary Blue" token="--brand-300" hex="#1D65AF" usage="Buttons, links, focus, active states, key accents." />

<ColorSwatch name="Deep Navy" token="--brand-400" hex="#193B62" usage="Headings on tinted ground, dark surfaces, selection on white." />

<ColorSwatch name="Hover Blue" token="--brand-200" hex="#69A2E5" usage="Hover and pressed states of the primary blue." />

<ColorSwatch name="Soft Tint" token="--brand-100" hex="#A3C0DF" usage="Selection highlight, tinted chip and callout backgrounds." />

</div>

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Brand-100 is a tint, not a solid.</span> In the source of truth (<span className="font-mono">spa-elfath-website/src/app/globals.css</span>) it is declared as an alpha: <span className="font-mono">rgba(163, 192, 223, 0.35)</span>. Overlay it on a surface rather than printing a rough hex approximation.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Never approximate.</span> If — and only if — the exact hex is unavailable, use the hsl equivalent of the same value; adjusting lightness to "make it pop" changes the brand.</p>

<SectionHeading id="neutrals" number="02" title="Warm Greys & Neutrals" />

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Grey is the structural system, blue is the accent.</span> If a design reads as "lots of blue", the palette is being used as decoration — step back to the greys and let the blue point at what matters.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Every step has a purpose.</span> Steps exist to make borders recede, text breathe, and surfaces separate. Two greys pressed side by side that look identical have an undefined job.</p>

</div>

<SectionHeading id="semantic" number="03" title="Semantic Colors" />

Semantic accents are reserved for status — success, warning, and danger. They are never decorative, and they never appear without a signal word or icon next to them:

<SectionHeading id="gradients" number="05" title="Gradients" />

The brand is flat on purpose — industrial, sharp, honest. Gradients that fake depth are the fastest way to lose the identity, so the rule is short: no multi-stop gradients, no mesh backgrounds, no text gradients.
