One voice needs one typeface. Every locale — Arabic, French, English — speaks in IBM Plex Sans Arabic, so a headline keeps its posture when the language changes underneath it.

<SectionHeading id="family" number="01" title="Family & Weights" />

Plex Arabic for all locales, loaded once, substituted never. The approved range runs 300 to 700 — Light lives in the range because production uses it for hero subtitles, not by accident:

<TypeDemo variant="weights" label="The approved range" note="300 Light through 700 Bold, as loaded. No Condensed, no Italic, no second family." />

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Never substitute without owner approval.</span> A “close enough” Arabic-capable family is a different voice — the lockup, the measure, and the numerals all shift with it.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Western digits everywhere.</span> Years, prices, phones, and stats set in Western numerals in all locales — no Arabic-Indic digits, and a number never reorders its digits:</p>

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">FR and EN may scale down, never up.</span> Where a translation overflows its layout, French and English set slightly smaller to fit — Arabic never shrinks to match, and nothing ever scales up past the rung.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">The eyebrow is a lockup, not a label.</span> <span className="font-mono">text-xs font-bold tracking-widest</span> in brand-300, preceded by the <span className="font-mono">h-1 w-6</span> brand dash — always paired, never restyled per section.</p>
