Buttons are the only voice that asks for something. Three variants ship in production — every class below is verbatim from `Button.tsx` — and this page adds the two rules production never wrote: a destructive treatment and icons in buttons. Rows tagged invented are guideline additions for future projects; everything else ships today.

<SectionHeading id="variants" number="01" title="Variants" />

One job each. Primary closes — hero CTAs, the quote form submit. Secondary offers — the alternative beside a primary. Ghost retreats — quiet actions inside dense layouts. All three share the base: `inline-flex items-center justify-center tracking-wide transition-colors duration-150`, sharp corners, Plex voice:

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Primary is singular.</span> One primary per view — the single thing the page wants. A second primary is a second promise; demote it to secondary.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Destructive is always the red outline — never solid red as a default.</span> Destruction must look reversible until committed: outline at rest, solid fill only on hover. Destructive never shares a row with primary; it sits apart, last, alone.</p>

<SectionHeading id="sizes" number="02" title="Sizes" />

Three heights ship — `sm h-8 px-4 text-xs`, `md h-10 px-5 text-sm`, `lg h-12 px-7 text-base` — and `md` is the default the component itself falls back to. The square icon-only companions are invented, one square per height:

<ButtonDemo variant="sizes" label="Heights at true size" tAction="Request a quote" note="sm / md / lg: Button.tsx verbatim, rendered at true height. Squares 32/40/48: INVENTED companions — same heights, zero horizontal padding, 20px glyph." />

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">lg is earned, not decorative.</span> Hero promises and form submits take `lg`; everything inline — table rows, card footers, nav bars — stays `md` or `sm`. A large button never decorates, it closes.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Touch never drops below 32.</span> The `sm` 32px height is the floor — anything smaller stops being a button and becomes a link. Icon-only squares keep the full 32/40/48 hit area; the glyph shrinks, the target never does.</p>

</div>

<SectionHeading id="states" number="03" title="States" />

Four states, no surprises. Hover lightens primary to brand-200; disabled locks at `opacity-40` with `cursor-not-allowed`; focus-visible draws the global ring — `2px solid brand-300` with `2px offset`, straight from `globals.css`:

<ButtonDemo variant="states" label="One button, four moments" tAction="Request a quote" note="Hover, disabled, and focus are live — tab through the specimens. The focus row pins the ring statically for reference." />

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Disabled explains itself.</span> A disabled button never stands alone — it keeps its label visible at 40% and the surrounding copy says why and what unlocks it. No tooltips-as-excuses, no disappearing act.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Focus is never removed.</span> The 2px ring with 2px offset is the keyboard user's cursor — restyling it is allowed, removing it is not. Every variant, including ghost and destructive, takes the same ring.</p>

</div>

<SectionHeading id="icons" number="04" title="Icons in Buttons" />

Production ships no icon rule — so this section is invented whole, from the icon and RTL foundations. Leading icons act (`Add01` + “Request a quote”); trailing directionals move (“Learn more” + arrow); squares stand alone. One glyph per button, always 20px, always `strokeWidth 1.5`, always `currentColor`:

<ButtonDemo variant="icons" label="Glyphs with passports" tAction="Request a quote" tMore="Learn more" note="INVENTED section. Directional glyphs mirror in RTL; the plus stays put. The RTL strip shows both rules live." />

<div className="not-prose mt-3 border-y border-[var(--hig-border)] py-3">

<p className="text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Directional mirrors, functional stays.</span> Arrows, chevrons, and “more” glyphs flip in RTL — they point where the reader goes. Plus, download, close, and status glyphs never flip — they mean, they don't point.</p>

<p className="mt-3 text-[13px] leading-6 text-[var(--apple-text)]"><span className="font-semibold">Icon-only is a last resort with a first-class label.</span> A square button always carries an `aria-label` in the reader's language — the glyph is seen, the label is spoken. Two icons in one button is a sentence; rewrite the label instead.</p>

</div>
